WHAT AM I BUILDING? (In Plain English)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Imagine this: You come home from grocery shopping. You want to know:
- "What do I have in my fridge?" 
- "When does it expire?"
- "What can I cook with these ingredients?"
- "How much food am I wasting anyway?"

My app does ALL of this. Here's how:

1. **Barcode Scanner:** You scan a barcode → app looks it up → tells you what it is
2. **Image Recognition:** You take a photo of your fridge → app recognizes the food
3. **Expiration Tracking:** You tell it when things expire → app reminds you when food is about to spoil
4. **Recipe Suggestions:** You see what you have → app suggests recipes you can make
5. **Impact Dashboard:** You see stats like "You've saved €25 and 4kg of CO2 this month"

And the best part? The app learns what you waste most, and helps you do better.

That's the VISION. But I need to be realistic about what I can actually build in 12 weeks 
as one person without being a Python expert.

So here's my actual plan:

WEEK 5-6: TRAINING MY AI MODEL - THE MACHINE TEACHING
═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

What I'm doing:
  □ Taking hundreds of photos of food (apples, bananas, bread, milk, tomatoes, etc.)
  □ Uploading them to Google's Teachable Machine
  □ Telling Teachable Machine: "This is an apple, this is a banana, this is milk"
  □ Having it train a model: "Learn to recognize these foods"
  □ Testing the model: "Does it correctly identify a NEW apple I show it?"
  □ Improving it: "Where does it fail? Can I fix it?"

Why this matters:
  This is the heart of my AI project. I'm literally TEACHING a machine. This is 
  machine learning! And Teachable Machine makes it simple - no PhD in ML required.

What I'm building:
  - A model that recognizes 7-10 food types
  - Goal: 80% accuracy (meaning 8 out of 10 times, it gets it right)
  - Will identify: Apples, Bananas, Bread, Milk, Tomatoes, Carrots, Lettuce
  - Optional: Cheese, Eggs, Yogurt (if I have time)

Data I need to collect:
  - 100+ photos per food category
  - Different angles, lighting, backgrounds
  - Mix of store-bought and homemade looking items
  - I can use my phone camera + images from Google Images

Deliverables by end of Week 6:
  ✓ Trained image recognition model (downloaded from Teachable Machine)
  ✓ Dataset documentation: "I collected X photos of Y category"
  ✓ Accuracy report: "My model is 78% accurate on unseen images"

WHAT COULD GO WRONG (& MY BACKUP PLANS)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Scenario 1: "It's Week 8 and I'm behind on development"
  → PLAN: Cut features ruthlessly. Focus on barcode scanning OR image recognition,
     not both. Spend saved time on documentation. Document WHY I cut features
     (this becomes evidence of investigative problem solving!)

Scenario 2: "My image recognition model is only 60% accurate"
  → PLAN: That's actually fine. Document it honestly. Analyze WHY it fails.
     Explain what I'd do differently. The learning journey matters more than
     the final accuracy.

Scenario 3: "I don't understand how to code the API integration"
  → PLAN: Use the code templates provided. Copy-paste and adapt. Watch tutorials.
     Ask ChatGPT. Ask friends. DOCUMENT my learning (becomes personal leadership
     evidence). If all fails, offer manual barcode entry as backup.

Scenario 4: "It's Week 11 and I haven't organized ANY evidence"
  → PLAN: Stop development. Spend 2-3 days organizing everything I've done.
     Better late than never. Be honest in reflections about late documentation.

Scenario 5: "No one wants to test my app"
  → PLAN: Ask differently. Not "Will you test my app?" but "I'm building this
     app - can you spend 10 minutes trying it and telling me what's confusing?"
     Offer coffee/snacks. Make it easy (10 min max).
